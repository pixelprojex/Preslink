# Fast, fair, transparent play earns repeat giving

Websites and casual games win attention in the first few seconds and keep it with instant, legible feedback on a goal people believe they can reach; people pay when the ask is small and concrete, the goal looks credible and close, payment takes seconds, and they can see where the money goes. The best-evidenced levers are unglamorous: a fast first screen (**53% of mobile visits are abandoned when a page takes more than three seconds**), short scannable copy, feedback within about 100 ms, small comparison groups with a reachable next target, a visibly seeded goal bar and wallet checkout. The most-hyped levers are the weakest: colour-psychology effects are small and context-bound, near-miss effects on persistence have not replicated, variable-reward "hooks" lack controlled tests in casual games, and habits take about two months to form, longer than PresLink's whole campaign. The pressure mechanics that do work (streaks, spend-linked status, urgency) are the ones EU, UK and Irish rules and guidance now discourage for services used by under-18s. PresLink Arcade already has most of the engagement machinery it needs. Its real risks are a "most plays" prize that rewards spending, no cap on what a pupil can spend, designed-in chance in who wins "best single light", and an unconfirmed position under Irish gaming and lottery law. Fixing them should cost little, because in comparable pay-to-play settings much of the money comes from a small tail of heavy spenders, the tail a school charity should not want from children (an inference from gambling and loot-box data, not a measured result for school games).

> **How to read the evidence.** None of the six research strands could open primary pages directly (a network proxy blocked them), so most figures come from abstracts, repository records and search-result extracts quoting the source, and search budgets ran out before some checks. The colour-and-visual-design and reward-loops strands were also cut short by a usage limit, so their coverage is thinner: notably on colour meanings such as gold and purple, on how teachers and parents read playful styling, and on the measured results of "juicy" feedback experiments. Below, *vendor* marks unaudited industry data, *second-hand* marks figures seen only in summaries, and *unverified* marks items a researcher recalled or could not open. Legal points are general information, not legal advice.

## Visitors decide in ten seconds, on speed and scanning

For a site reached from a QR poster, the first load is the whole funnel. Google and SOASTA found that **53% of mobile visits are abandoned when a page takes longer than three seconds**, and that bounce probability rises 32% as load time grows from one second to three ([Think with Google](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/intl/en-gb/marketing-strategies/app-and-mobile/mobile-page-speed-new-industry-benchmarks); [Think with Google data](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/data/page-load-time-statistics)). That is vendor data, but controlled tests agree on direction. Google's 2009 experiment added 100–400 ms of delay to search results and cut searches per user by 0.2–0.6%, an effect that grew with exposure and lingered after the delay was removed ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/speed-matters/)). Vodafone's A/B test of an otherwise identical page that rendered 31% faster produced **8% more sales** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/case-studies/vodafone)). For web games, download size is the lever: the portal Poki found the share of players who stay while a game loads falls with file size, to about 80% at 10 MB for US players, and treats 65% "conversion to play" and five minutes' average playtime as signs a game works (practitioner) ([W3C workshop slides](https://www.w3.org/2018/12/games-workshop/slides/12-size-matters.pdf); [Defold/Poki guide](https://defold.com/extension-poki-sdk/best-practices)).

Judgement arrives before reading. Appeal ratings after a 50 ms glimpse match ratings after longer study ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/news/2006/060109/full/news060109-13.html)). Visual complexity lowers appeal within 17–50 ms even when a layout is familiar ([Tuch et al. 2012](https://research.google/pubs/pub38315/)), and complexity, colourfulness, age and education together explain about half the variance in appeal after 500 ms, with first impressions carrying into later judgements of usability and trustworthiness ([Reinecke et al. 2013](https://kgajos.seas.harvard.edu/papers/reinecke13aesthetics.pdf)). Microsoft's analysis of over two billion visit durations found people screen a page before engaging with it, which NN/g reads as making **the first 10 seconds** the stay-or-leave window; entertaining pages are screened less harshly ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/); [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/understanding-web-browsing-behaviors-through-weibull-analysis-of-dwell-time/)). Attention also stays near the top: in NN/g's 2018 desktop eye-tracking, 57% of viewing time fell above the fold and 74% within two screenfuls; no phone equivalent was found ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)).

People scan rather than read. In Nielsen's classic tests **79% of users scanned and 16% read word by word**, and a page rewritten to be concise, scannable and objective scored 124% higher on measured usability, a single-site 1997 result whose size should not be generalised ([Nielsen & Morkes, mirror](https://www.deceptive.design/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web)). Timing data suggest visitors read about **20% of the words** on an average page ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-little-do-users-read/)), numerals catch scanning eyes ([IntMath on NN/g](https://www.intmath.com/blog/mathematics/writing-numbers-on-the-web-610)), and the F-pattern, in which first lines and the first words of each line take most fixations, persists on phones ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/)). Difficult text suffers most on small screens: one study (second-hand, contested) put comprehension on an iPhone-sized screen at 48% of desktop, while NN/g's 2016 follow-up found short, simple text understood equally well on both ([NN/g 2011](https://nngroup.com/articles/mobile-content-is-twice-as-difficult-2011); [Wylie summary](https://www.wyliecomm.com/2023/05/reading-on-mobile)). This audience raises the stakes. NN/g found teenagers confident but less successful than adults (55% against 66% task success in its first teen study), held back by "lower reading levels, impatience, and undeveloped research skills" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-of-websites-for-teenagers/)); **21% of Irish adults score at or below Level 1 literacy** ([CSO, PIAAC 2023](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-piaac/programmefortheinternationalassessmentofadultcompetenciespiaac2023/keyfindings)); and NN/g's long-standing advice is roughly a 6th-grade reading level on landing pages ([Nielsen 2005, library record](https://atjlibrary.org/node/441/article-lower-literacy-users-writing-broad-consumer-audience-nielsen-2005)). Phones are held one-handed about half the time (49% in a 2013 observation of 1,333 people, before today's larger phones), so the main action belongs large and within thumb reach ([Garcia Media on Hoober](https://garciamedia.com/?p=7518); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/touch-target-size/)).

A few-week campaign cannot rely on habit. The best-known estimate puts automaticity at a median **66 days** (range 18–254), from one small, unreplicated study ([BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit)), and the median mobile game keeps about 22% of players to day one and under 4% to day seven (vendor) ([GameDev Reports on GameAnalytics](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game)). Returns have to be prompted. The closest rigorous analogue to school posters is encouraging: signs at the point of decision raised stair use by a median 2.4 percentage points, **a 50% relative increase**, across 21 study arms ([The Community Guide](https://www.thecommunityguide.org/findings/physical-activity-point-decision-prompts-encourage-use-stairs)). Digital prompts are weaker and carry costs. Nonprofit fundraising emails average a 0.48% click rate ([M+R Benchmarks](https://mrbenchmarks.com/email)); charity reminders raise donations but also unsubscriptions, and ignoring that annoyance overstates the welfare gain tenfold ([Damgaard & Gravert](https://swopec.hhs.se/gunwpe/abs/gunwpe0650.htm)); and more frequent push messages raised uninstalls in a 17,500-user field experiment ([Wohllebe et al.](https://www.businessperspectives.org/index.php/journals/innovative-marketing/issue-382/mobile-apps-in-retail-effect-of-push-notification-frequency-on-app-user-behavior)). Time on site is the wrong target for a game where every replay costs €2; the conclusion sets out better measures.

## Colour nudges mood faintly; clutter, contrast and motion decide use

This strand was cut short, so its coverage is narrower than the others, but its core finding is clear: popular colour psychology rests on thin ground. The field's own reviewer describes the literature as "at a nascent stage of development", much of it poorly controlled ([Elliot 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00368/full)). What holds up is narrow. Red raises arousal, but its emotional meaning depends on context: in one lab study red made test-related pictures feel more negative ([Buechner & Maier 2016](https://peerj.com/articles/2515)), and the claim that red impairs intellectual performance now looks close to zero after four replication attempts ([Collabra](https://online.ucpress.edu/collabra/article/6/1/3/113047/Processing-the-Word-Red-and-Intellectual)). In branding, red maps to excitement and blue to competence, and the payoff comes from a fit between colour and intended personality rather than from any winning hue ([Labrecque & Milne](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/251277565_Exciting_red_and_competent_blue_the_importance_of_color_in_marketing)). Colour–emotion associations are strongly shared across 4,598 people in 30 nations (pattern similarity r = .88), though that measures associations, not behaviour ([Jonauskaite et al. 2020](https://biopen.bi.no/bi-xmlui/bitstream/11250/2761614/1/Jonauskaite_etal_2020_PsychScience.pdf)). The ubiquitous claim that **colour raises brand recognition by 80% has no traceable primary study** ([insights4print](https://www.insights4print.ceo/2019/02/color-increases-brand-recognition-by-80-the-real-contents-of-the-loyola-study-revealed/)). No evidence was found either way on gold for reward or purple for prestige, and the often-quoted "red button beat green by 21%" test could not be verified.

Structure, not hue, changes behaviour. Complexity outweighs colourfulness in first impressions ([Reinecke et al.](https://kgajos.seas.harvard.edu/papers/reinecke13aesthetics.pdf)), and young adults miss key information unless a page offers "a limited number of clear focal points" above the fold ([Djamasbi et al. 2011](https://www.ijec-web.org/past-issues/volume-15-number-4-summer-2011/online-viewing-and-aesthetic-preferences-of-generation-y-and-the-baby-boom-generation-testing-user-web-site-experience-through-eye-tracking/)). Flat buttons that lose contrast or edges cost attention: in NN/g's 71-person eye-tracking study, weak clickability cues meant **22% more time and 25% more fixations** on findability tasks ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/flat-ui-less-attention-cause-uncertainty/)). The aesthetic-usability effect is real for impressions, with more attractive ATM layouts rated easier to use (r = 0.59) ([Kurosu & Kashimura](https://chi1995.chistatic.hosting.acm.org/proceedings/shortppr/mk_bdy.htm)), but the best-controlled test reversed the arrow. In an 80-person experiment crossing high and low aesthetics with high and low usability, beauty did not change perceived usability, while poor usability made a site look worse through frustration ([Tuch et al. 2012](https://research.google/pubs/is-beautiful-really-usable-toward-understanding-the-relation-between-usability-aesthetics-and-affect-in-hci/)). Polish buys the first glance; lag and confusion spend it. Duolingo's bright, rounded look is often cited as proof that colourful design engages, but Duolingo's own account gives clarity and speed of production as the reasons and publishes no engagement figures ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/shape-language-duolingos-art-style/)). Nothing retrieved tests whether teachers or parents read a bright palette as childish, the largest gap for this audience.

Legibility, by contrast, has hard floors. WCAG requires **4.5:1 contrast for body text and 3:1 for large text and for the visual cues that show a control exists** ([W3C 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html); [W3C 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)), colour must never be the only signal ([W3C 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/use-of-color.html)), and red text on dark backgrounds is a specific hazard for colour-blind readers. Platform guidance converges on 16–17 px body text ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography); [Material](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Typography.md)), 50–75 characters a line ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/line-length-readability)) and tap targets of at least 24 px, with 44–48 px recommended ([W3C 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). Dark text on light backgrounds beat dark mode on acuity and proofreading for young and older adults alike, and the advantage grew as text shrank (Piepenbrock et al., via an NN/g summary) ([summary](https://gi-radar.de/tl/DC-0313)). Motion is the strongest attention-grabber and so the easiest to misuse. NN/g, Google and Apple converge on 100–500 ms for interface animation, with transitions beyond about 400 ms feeling slow and motion kept purposeful, brief and cancellable ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/); [Material](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Motion.md); [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)). WCAG adds a pause control for anything that moves automatically for more than five seconds, a limit of three flashes a second and respect for the reduce-motion setting ([W3C 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html); [W3C 2.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html); [W3C 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). Google's accessibility course says over 35% of adults have had some vestibular dysfunction by age 40 (primary study not opened) ([web.dev](https://web.dev/learn/accessibility/motion)), which covers many of the arcade's parents and teachers.

## Instant, truthful feedback beats engineered near-misses

This strand was also cut short: it could not read several key experiments' results, and some classic findings below were cited from memory. PresLink's core loop belongs to a genre that retains well: among top hyper-casual games in GameAnalytics' 2020 breakdown, **timing games had the highest day-one retention, at 44%** (vendor) ([GameAnalytics](https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/the-metrics-behind-hyper-casual-games-industry-report)). The mechanic needs execution more than extra systems, and execution starts with latency. In experiments on touchscreen buttons, perceived quality fell once feedback lagged by **70–100 ms for touch or sound and 100–150 ms for visuals**, and inconsistent delay hurt satisfaction ([Kaaresoja et al. 2014](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/104653)). A 2021 study reports that redundant "juicy" vibration, layered on visual and audio feedback, can improve enjoyment and immersion ([Waterloo Haptic Experience Lab](https://uwaterloo.ca/haptic-experience-lab/projects/juicy-haptic-design-vibrotactile-embellishments-can-improve)), but the results of the main 2019 juiciness experiment could not be read, so the evidence cannot say where more juice stops helping ([Hicks et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1145/3311350.3347171)). Safari on iPhones does not support web vibration (unverified this session), so sound and visuals must carry the feel there.

Near-misses need the most care. Among 60 Candy Crush players, near-misses were more arousing than losses, the most frustrating outcome of all, and produced **the strongest urge to keep playing** ([Larche et al. 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10899-016-9633-7)); a 15-person brain-imaging study found near-misses raised the desire to play only when players felt in control ([Clark et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2658737/)). Yet a 2019 review with three new experiments found **no replicable evidence that near-misses make gamblers persist longer** ([Pisklak et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7214505)). Clark drew the distinction that matters for a skill game: in skill tasks a near-miss is information about how to adjust, whereas in gambling it means nothing ([ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2009/02/090211122130.htm)). The dark version is celebrating failure. Slot-machine "losses disguised as wins", payouts below the stake dressed in win sounds, produced physiological responses as large as real wins ([Dixon et al. 2010](https://uwaterloo.ca/reasoning-decision-making-lab/sites/default/files/uploads/files/DixFugetal_10c.pdf)), and with sound on, gamblers overestimated their wins by 24% against 15% with sound off ([University of Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/news/news/music-gamblers-ears)).

Progress and reference points have stronger support than reward schedules. People speed up as they near a reward, and a car-wash card with two free stamps was completed by 34% of customers against 19% for an equivalent card without them; both are classic studies cited from memory and not re-opened ([Kivetz et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39); [Nunes & Drèze 2006](https://doi.org/10.1086/500480)). Difficulty should err easy when the game sets it: across more than 20,000 sessions of an educational game, players who chose their level liked moderate challenge, but when difficulty was assigned the easiest versions motivated most, and suspense in close games helped ([Lomas et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1145/3025453.3025638); [CMU HCII](https://hcii.cmu.edu/news/chi-preview-researchers-hcii-believe-difficulty-can-be-overrated-games)). Scales matter too: differences look bigger on scales with more units, so a gain on 0–1,000 feels larger than the same gain on 0–10 ([Pandelaere et al. 2011](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/doi10.1086-659000.html)). Streaks are the best-evidenced return mechanic: an intact streak shown in a log becomes a goal in itself, and highlighting a broken one reduces engagement ([Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/42ce576b-8e1f-429a-8541-e29e48dbcfbb/full)); Duolingo reports that a streak-milestone animation raised new learners' odds of being active on day seven by 1.7% (company figure, no method published) ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)). That power comes from fear of loss, which is why the teen-safety section treats streaks as off-limits for pupils. Variable-reward "hook" models remain practitioner doctrine without controlled tests in casual games, and Wordle's one-puzzle-a-day limit comes with a designer's rationale but no published retention data ([NYT via Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/national-news/2022/01/04/he-made-wordle-for-his-partner-now-its-an-online-hit/)).

## Leaderboards motivate only those who can close the gap

Leaderboards work by turning other people's scores into goals. In a lab experiment, a leaderboard alone pushed performance to the level of people given difficult or impossible goals ([Landers et al. 2017](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/gamification-of-task-performance-with-leaderboards-a-goal-setting/)), and a 2025 replication found boards lift speed and quantity more than accuracy ([Kratochvíl et al.](https://www.muni.cz/en/research/publications/2495997)). Whether the gap can be closed decides the sign of the effect. When MovieLens told members the median number of ratings, those below it **raised contributions by 530% while those above cut theirs by 62%** ([Chen et al. 2010](https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/aecrev/v100y2010i4p1358-98.html)), and people competing against 10 rivals finished a quiz faster than those facing 100 ([Garcia & Tor 2009](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~smgarcia/pubs/n-effect.pdf)). In semester-long online courses, absolute public boards energised the top while middle and bottom students reported growing pressure; the bottom third preferred anonymity on absolute boards, and the authors recommend relative "around me" boards and limiting public display of low ranks ([Bai et al. 2021](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/96762); [Bai & Hew 2025](https://hub.hku.hk/handle/10722/368255)). The standing caution: a course section given a leaderboard and badges saw motivation, satisfaction and exam scores fall over the term ([Hanus & Fox 2015](https://www.smhp.psych.ucla.edu/pdfdocs/gamil.pdf)). Meta-analysis puts gamification's effects at small to moderate (g = .25 to .49), with **competition combined with collaboration** the most effective arrangement ([Sailer & Homner 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)), and in a walking-app field test social comparison added nothing beyond plain self-progress feedback ([Zuckerman & Gal-Oz](https://runi.ac.il/media/whrh3e33/deconstructing-gamification.pdf)). Almost all of this evidence comes from adults and university students; no leaderboard study of 12–18-year-olds in a fundraising setting was found.

Industry practice follows the same logic of small, time-boxed, matched groups. Duolingo's weekly leagues match learners by habits and time zone ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-leagues-leaderboards/)), and its former product chief credits leaderboards, alongside streaks and notifications, with a 4.5-fold rise in daily users over four years ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)); the widely repeated "+17% learning time" figure is second-hand. Strava added Local Legends, awarded for the most completions of a segment in 90 days, because speed boards were out of reach for most riders ([BikeRadar](https://bikeradar.com/news/strava-local-legend)). That is the nearest precedent for PresLink's "most plays" prize, with one decisive difference: Strava's effort costs nothing. Wordle's growth is credited to a share grid that boasts without spoiling ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/05/04/wordle-new-york-times-user-growth)), and three-letter arcade initials date from 1979, so initials-only identity reads as genre convention rather than a privacy compromise ([Arcade Blogger](https://arcadeblogger.com/2021/01/31/anatomy-of-arcade-high-score-tables/)).

Information about other people's giving cuts both ways. Telling public-radio callers that another donor had given an amount at the **90th–95th percentile** raised gifts from new donors but not from renewing ones ([Shang & Croson 2009](https://econweb.ucsd.edu/~jandreon/PhilanthropyAndFundraising/Volume%202/27%20Shang%20Croson%202009.pdf)), and a replication with mostly renewing members found nothing ([NVSQ](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0899764019869537)). Across 300,000 London Marathon donations, a £10 rise in the average of visible earlier gifts raised the next gift by about £2.50, while **one visible small gift lowered later gifts by about £5** ([Smith, Windmeijer & Wright](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/cmpo/publications/papers/2013/abstract302.html)). Local norms beat global ones ([Agerström et al. 2016](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/joepsy/v52y2016icp147-153.html)), and social influence travels mainly through close friends ([Bond et al. 2012](https://sparq.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj19021/files/media/file/bond_et_al._2012_-_a_61-million-person_experiment_in_social_influence_political_mobilization.pdf)). Being seen to give raises giving ([Karlan & McConnell](https://nber.org/papers/w17737)), but monetary rewards work worse, and can backfire, when the good deed is public ([Ariely, Bracha & Meier](https://www.bostonfed.org/publications/research-department-working-paper/2007/doing-good-or-doing-well-image-motivation-and-monetary-incentives-in-behaving-prosocially.aspx)). Prize lotteries raise participation most of all: a single-prize lottery drew contributions from 17.1% of households against 9.5% for a plain ask ([Landry et al. 2006](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w11611/w11611.pdf); [re-analysis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.02276)), which is exactly why Irish law licenses them.

## Seeded goals, high anchors and wallet checkout move small gifts

PresLink's €2, 3-for-€5 and 7-for-€10 menu works as an ask ladder, and the evidence on ladders describes a trade-off rather than a free lunch. Low defaults raise the share who give and lower the average gift, and high defaults do the reverse ([Goswami & Urminsky 2016](https://researchconnect.suny.edu/en/publications/when-should-the-ask-be-a-nudge-the-effect-of-default-amounts-on-c/)); on a large donation website, defaults pulled the most common gift to the suggested amount without changing total donations ([Altmann et al.](https://docs.iza.org/dp8680.pdf)). Low suggested amounts drag payments down more than high ones lift them ([Chronicle of Philanthropy](https://www.philanthropy.com/news/suggesting-low-gift-amounts-can-drag-down-fundraising-results-study-finds/)). "Even a small amount helps" framing raises compliance (r = .22) with similar or higher totals ([meta-analysis](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/the-legitimization-of-paltry-contributions-as-a-compliance-gainin/)). Bundle advice, a round base price plus two better-value tiers, is vendor opinion only ([RallyUp](https://rallyup.com/blog/how-to-price-raffle-tickets/)); the defensible claim is that bundles raise order value and shrink the share lost to per-transaction fees, not that they raise the share who pay.

The "raised so far" ring is the best-supported element of the design, on two conditions. Giving accelerates as a fund nears its goal because late gifts feel decisive ([Cryder et al. 2013](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/GoalGradient.pdf)); across 25,058 Kickstarter projects, support rose near the goal, most strongly near the deadline and for small goals, and **fell once the goal was reached** ([Kuppuswamy & Bayus 2017](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbvent/v32y2017i1p72-89.html)). The mirror image is the empty bar: announcing seed money of 67% rather than 10% of a goal produced a **nearly six-fold increase in contributions** ([List & Lucking-Reiley 2002](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/ucpjpolec/v_3a110_3ay_3a2002_3ai_3a1_3ap_3a215-233.htm)), and crowdfunding campaigns that stall mid-way rarely recover without a new spark ([Maryland Smith](https://www.rhsmith.umd.edu/research/how-have-successful-crowdfunding-campaign)). A sponsor's match raises both response and revenue, and 1:1 does as well as 2:1 or 3:1 ([Karlan & List 2007](https://isps.yale.edu/sites/default/files/publication/2012/12/AER%2C%202007%2C%20Karlan%2C%20DoesPriceMatter.pdf)); the often-quoted +19% revenue and +22% response were recalled rather than re-verified.

Concrete, bounded impact beats emotional imagery and statistics. The famous identifiable-victim effect shrinks to r = .05 in meta-analysis and to about zero after correcting for publication bias, and a preregistered replication of the classic study failed ([Lee & Feeley meta-analysis](https://pure.skku.edu/en/publications/the-identifiable-victim-effect-a-meta-analytic-review/); [FORRT](https://forrt.org/flora-replication-atlas/doi/10.1016/j.obhdp.2006.01.005/)). Adding scientific impact evidence to letters sent to 21,643 donors changed nothing on average, and small past donors gave less ([Karlan & Wood](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20047.pdf)). What survives is tangibility: asking "how much for one?" before "how much for all?" raised totals in replications, though as a one-off boost ([APS Observer](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/observer/obsonline/bringing-in-more-donations-to-the-cause-at-no-extra-cost.html); [LSE](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/119836)), and people value solving a large share of a bounded problem over a sliver of a vast one ([Frontiers](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.800867/full)). Where the money goes matters more than pictures. Donors told that overheads were already covered were **80% more likely to give** ([Gneezy et al. 2014](https://Rady.ucsd.edu/_files/faculty-research/uri-gneezy/Science-2014-Gneezy-632-5.pdf)), and in Ireland a lack of transparency about where money goes is the main reason given for declining trust in charities, with 90% saying clear information on use and results would raise it ([Activelink](https://www.activelink.ie/node/116383); [Charities Regulator](https://www.charitiesregulator.ie/ga/faisn%C3%A9is-don-phobal/%C3%A1r-nuacht/2023/may/nine-out-of-10-irish-adults-donated-to-charity-last-year)).

Paying on a phone is where small gifts leak. On nonprofit donation pages, 8% of mobile visitors completed a gift against 11% on desktop, and just 4% for small nonprofits ([M+R 2025](https://mrbenchmarks.com/website-performance/)). Stripe's aggregated tests report roughly a **10% conversion lift from adding Apple Pay**, with wallet checkouts finishing about three times faster (vendor) ([Stripe Sessions 2024](https://stripe.com/sessions/2024/a-look-under-stripes-hood-using-data-to-demystify-payments)), and Baymard's surveys attribute 19% of abandonments to distrust of the site with card details and 19% to forced account creation (second-hand figures) ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)). Deadlines change when people give, not whether: among 53,000 Danish donors, nearly all gifts arrived within two to three days of a message whatever the deadline, while a reminder raised both the likelihood and the size of gifts ([Damgaard & Gravert 2017](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/soceco/v66y2017icp78-87.html)).

## For teenagers, the risk is spending pressure, not boredom

Regulators have converged on what not to do to under-18s. The European Commission's July 2025 guidelines under the Digital Services Act recommend switching off by default "features that contribute to excessive use, like communication 'streaks,' ... autoplay, or push notifications" and removing persuasive design aimed mainly at engagement ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors)). They are non-binding and exclude micro and small enterprises, so for PresLink they are a benchmark rather than law ([IRIS Merlin](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/article/10383)). EU consumer authorities' March 2025 principles on in-game spending ask for clear real-money prices, no designs that hide costs or push purchases, and protection of children ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/en/perspectives/2025/03/qas-on-the-eu-consumer-protection-authorities-joint-guidance-paper)). Several rules likely to bind an Irish site could not be verified this session: the EU ban on "direct exhortations to children to buy" ([Unfair Commercial Practices Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2005/29/oj)), Ireland's digital age of consent of 16 ([Data Protection Act 2018, s.31](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2018/act/7/section/31/enacted/en/html)) and the Data Protection Commission's child-first "Fundamentals" ([DPC](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/dpc-guidance/fundamentals-child-oriented-approach-data-processing)).

The harm evidence comes mostly from gambling and loot boxes and transfers by analogy. Among 1,155 16–18-year-olds, loot-box spending was linked to problem-gambling severity with a moderate-to-large effect, **stronger than in adults**, though the direction of cause is unknown ([Zendle et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6599795)). In large experiments, mild dark patterns doubled the odds of people signing up for an unwanted paid plan and aggressive ones nearly quadrupled them; only the aggressive ones provoked backlash, and less-educated people were more susceptible ([Luguri & Strahilevitz 2021](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3431205)). Asking people to limit themselves barely works: 0.71% of online gamblers set a limit after an encouraging message against 0.08% of controls ([Heirene & Gainsbury, via iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/sustainable-gambling/responsible-gambling/messaging-about-limits-helps-reduce-net-losses-study-claims/)), and a randomised trial found deposit-limit prompts did not reduce net losses ([Ivanova et al. 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6455077/)). The evidence favours limits built into the product over invitations to self-limit. School rules point the same way. Department of Education guidance on voluntary contributions requires it to be "absolutely clear" that payment is not compulsory and bars charging for activities all pupils are expected to join ([Oireachtas](https://www.oireachtas.ie/en/debates/question/2018-07-03/178/)), and 80% of respondents to a 2023 St Vincent de Paul survey felt "voluntary" school contributions were not presented as optional ([Irish Farmers Journal](https://www.farmersjournal.ie/finance/consumer/parents-feel-the-voluntary-contribution-fee-pressure-877812)). Against that background, a spot prize for **most plays** is, in substance, a prize for spending most. The teen-safety research rates it the feature most at odds with consumer and minors' guidance, and it collides with the leaderboard evidence favouring effort awards, because at PresLink effort is spend.

The legal question is pivotal and unresolved; what follows is general information, not legal advice. Ireland's 1956 Act defines "gaming" as playing a game "whether of skill or chance or partly of skill and partly of chance" for stakes, where a stake includes any payment to take part ([Gaming and Lotteries Act 1956, s.2](https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/1956/act/2/section/2/revised/en/html)). Lotteries need a Garda permit (prizes up to €5,000, tickets up to €10) or a court licence ([McCann FitzGerald](https://www.mccannfitzgerald.com/knowledge/betting-and-gaming/the-gaming-and-lotteries-amendment-act-2019)). The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 exempts small charitable lotteries with winnings up to €2,000, entry up to €5 and at most 1,500 tickets ([Irish Statute Book, s.92](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/2024/en/act/pub/0035/sec0092.html)), a provision one strand read from the statute and another flagged as unverified, and whose commencement is unconfirmed. The regulator said its charitable licence would not open in 2025, and an August 2026 report said it still had not, leaving the old permit routes in force ([GRAI](https://www.grai.ie/licensing-regulation/charitable-philanthropic); [Connaught Telegraph](https://www.con-telegraph.ie/2026/08/20/what-the-new-gambling-law-means-for-your-club-lottery/)). If any part of the arcade counted as gaming or a lottery, under-18s would likely be excluded altogether, given an age floor of 18 under the 2019 amendment that was not verified this session ([2019 Act](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2019/act/44/enacted/en/html)). Britain's test, that skill must stop a "significant proportion" of entrants from winning, states the principle most clearly ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/prize-competitions-and-free-prize-draws-new-guidance-1)). A tap-timing game is skill on its face, but the current build gives each light a random mark position, and its own code notes that a 999 needs a whole-millisecond tap to land within a few microseconds of a randomly placed line. A cautious reader could call that chance, and it sits inside the "best single light" prize. The research notes also cite a random sweep speed, but the code has used a fixed one-second sweep since migration `0014`.

## Conclusion

For a paid charity game played partly by children, the evidence turns the standard engagement playbook upside down. The levers with the strongest support (speed, clarity, instant truthful feedback, reachable goals, seeded progress, frictionless payment and specific transparency) are also the ethically clean ones. The levers with weak or failed evidence (colour psychology, engineered near-misses, variable rewards) offer little, and the pressure mechanics that genuinely work (streaks, spend-linked status, manufactured urgency) are the ones regulators are moving against. On most decisions PresLink faces no trade-off between raising money and treating pupils well. The honest exception is that removing mild pressure will lose some conversions, as the dark-pattern experiments show; in a closed school community whose players are the charity's supporters for every future campaign, that is a price worth paying.

The second implication concerns measurement. A few hundred payers over a few weeks cannot power conclusive A/B tests, so the committee should set defaults the evidence supports and watch a handful of numbers, most of which the database already holds: bundle mix, plays per player (especially among the heaviest spenders), payments in the three days after each poster refresh or announcement, and, if a simple visit counter is added, conversion from scan to first play. That also avoids adding behavioural tracking that Ireland's child-focused data-protection guidance makes hard to justify. The questions that matter most for this campaign are no longer design questions: whether the school's solicitor or the regulator accepts the arcade as a pure game of skill, and whether the school community experiences it as a voluntary bit of fun rather than one more ask.

## Recommendations for PresLink Arcade

Keep what the build already does in line with the evidence: no sign-up before play, hosted wallet checkout, taps timed at the moment of touch, early/late coaching, a fixed one-second sweep, separate boards, a "points to the next place" line, initials-only identity, and confetti only for 850+ averages and never under reduced motion. Items run in priority order; evidence strength is in brackets.

### Do now

1. **Get a written view of the arcade's legal status before taking real money.** Ask the school's solicitor, or write to the GRAI or the local Garda licensing office: is a paid timing game with prizes, open to under-18s, outside "gaming" and "lottery" under the 1956 Act as amended and the 2024 Act? Check Stripe's terms for contests at the same time ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses), unverified). The 1956 definitions reach games "partly of skill and partly of chance", and if the arcade were caught, under-18s would likely be barred ([1956 Act](https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/1956/act/2/section/2/revised/en/html); [GRAI](https://www.grai.ie/licensing-regulation/charitable-philanthropic)). [law; status unverified]
2. **Replace the "most plays" spot prize with an award money cannot buy**, such as most improved average, best light of each week, or a reward the whole school unlocks at a fundraising milestone; do not swap in a random draw without a lottery permit. A plays board is a spending board, because rankings move quantity most ([Kratochvíl et al.](https://www.muni.cz/en/research/publications/2495997)), and rewards that push minors to spend are what EU consumer and minors' guidance discourages ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/en/perspectives/2025/03/qas-on-the-eu-consumer-protection-authorities-joint-guidance-paper); [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors)). [regulatory guidance; moderate]
3. **Take designed-in chance out of who wins.** Snap each mark to the same 1 ms grid as taps, so a perfect tap can score 999 on any light; better still, give every player the same marks each day, so every play is an identical test. Keep "earlier score wins" for ties, publish the scoring rule in plain words, and say that phones differ slightly in touch timing. This removes the "partly chance" argument and clears Britain's skill test easily ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/prize-competitions-and-free-prize-draws-new-guidance-1)), and lawsuits over rigged "skill" prize machines show how fast hidden variance destroys trust ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/input/gaming/segas-key-master-arcade-game-is-so-rigged-people-are-suing-the-company)). [law-adjacent; practitioner]
4. **Build in spending limits and show the running total.** Set a daily cap and a campaign cap for student accounts, and show "You've spent €X" on the You tab and before checkout. The figures are the committee's call (a daily cap of at least one 7-play bundle keeps bundles useful); the evidence supports a hard cap, not a particular number. Invitations to self-limit reach under 1% of people and did not cut losses in a randomised trial ([Heirene & Gainsbury](https://igamingbusiness.com/sustainable-gambling/responsible-gambling/messaging-about-limits-helps-reduce-net-losses-study-claims/); [Ivanova et al.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6455077/)), and spending on chance-based rewards tracks problem gambling more strongly in 16–18-year-olds than in adults ([Zendle et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6599795)). [strong for the principle; analogy from gambling]
5. **Open the ring with real seed money and use stepped goals.** Launch with a sponsor's, parents' association's or staff pledge already showing (never leftover demo plays, which the README says to clear), make the first goal reachable in week one, and announce the next goal the day it is passed. Seed money raised contributions nearly six-fold ([List & Lucking-Reiley](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/ucpjpolec/v_3a110_3ay_3a2002_3ai_3a1_3ap_3a215-233.htm)), and giving speeds up near a goal and drops after it ([Kuppuswamy & Bayus](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbvent/v32y2017i1p72-89.html)). [strong]
6. **Put one concrete impact line and the money trail on the Play screen:** what €2 pays for in the Ghana and Nigeria projects (a real figure from the beneficiaries), the net per play after Stripe's fee, PresLink's registered name and number if it has them, and a named adult contact, with detail behind a link. Covered-overhead framing raised the likelihood of giving by 80% ([Gneezy et al.](https://Rady.ucsd.edu/_files/faculty-research/uri-gneezy/Science-2014-Gneezy-632-5.pdf)), per-unit asks raise totals ([APS Observer](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/observer/obsonline/bringing-in-more-donations-to-the-cause-at-no-extra-cost.html)), and transparency about where money goes is the top driver of charity trust in Ireland ([Activelink](https://www.activelink.ie/node/116383)). [strong]
7. **Plan three prompt bursts instead of a drip:** launch, a mid-campaign spark (a new goal, a match day or a new prize) and the final three days. Put QR posters where phones come out (canteen queue, gate, sports hall) with a headline figure refreshed weekly, and send parents one or two messages in total, none to pupils. Point-of-decision signs lifted behaviour 50% ([Community Guide](https://www.thecommunityguide.org/findings/physical-activity-point-decision-prompts-encourage-use-stairs)), gifts arrive within days of a prompt ([Damgaard & Gravert 2017](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/soceco/v66y2017icp78-87.html)), and each extra reminder costs unsubscribes and goodwill ([Damgaard & Gravert 2018](https://swopec.hhs.se/gunwpe/abs/gunwpe0650.htm)). [strong]
8. **Make the email request suitable for children:** one plain sentence on what the email is for, who sees it and when it is deleted; a suggestion to use a parent's email for under-16s; an empty box; and no standing reminder row after "Not now", since the four-digit code already lets winners claim. Ireland's digital age of consent is 16, and DPC and ICO guidance discourage nudging children to hand over data ([DPA 2018, s.31](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2018/act/7/section/31/enacted/en/html); [DPC](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/dpc-guidance/fundamentals-child-oriented-approach-data-processing)). [law and regulator guidance; unverified this session]
9. **Run one pre-launch quality pass:** first screen within about a second and playable within three on a mid-range Android phone over 4G; Apple Pay, Google Pay and Link switched on in the live Stripe account; initials text on all eight tile colours at 4.5:1 (3:1 if large and bold); the orange light and ring at 3:1 against their backgrounds; no board or prize state shown by colour alone; body text of at least 16 px and tap targets of at least 44 px ([Think with Google](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/intl/en-gb/marketing-strategies/app-and-mobile/mobile-page-speed-new-industry-benchmarks); [Stripe](https://stripe.com/sessions/2024/a-look-under-stripes-hood-using-data-to-demystify-payments), vendor; [W3C 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html); [W3C 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)). [moderate to strong]

### Consider

1. **A free, unscored practice light** outside the prizes, so first-timers learn before paying, pupils who cannot pay can join in without being marked out, and each phone's touch timing can be checked. Letting people reach value before any barrier raised Duolingo's daily users about 20% (company-reported) ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/the-tenets-of-a-b-testing-from-duolingos-master-growth-hacker)), and school guidance warns against charging for what all pupils are expected to join ([Oireachtas](https://www.oireachtas.ie/en/debates/question/2018-07-03/178/)). Ask the solicitor whether a free route also strengthens the prize's legal footing. [moderate]
2. **Personal-best tension and honest celebration:** "Need 905 on light 3 to beat your best", the timing error in milliseconds beside the early/late coaching, a celebration for any new personal best even below 850, and an end screen that opens with the best true fact. Self-progress feedback matched social comparison in a field test ([Zuckerman & Gal-Oz](https://runi.ac.il/media/whrh3e33/deconstructing-gamification.pdf)) and close games build suspense ([Lomas et al.](https://doi.org/10.1145/3025453.3025638)); this draws on the cut-short reward-loops strand. [moderate]
3. **Soften the paid replay at the moment of peak urge.** When credits run out, lead the result screen with the coaching and the board, and keep "Play again · €2" available but secondary, without bundle links beside a poor result. Near-misses create the strongest urge to continue ([Larche et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10899-016-9633-7)); expect to lose some conversions ([Luguri & Strahilevitz](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3431205)). [moderate; judgement]
4. **Smaller, fairer comparison:** a "best light this week" board, each player's gap shown on their own board (Students or Teachers) rather than on Everyone, and a public top 10–20 with each player's own rank shown privately ([Garcia & Tor](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~smgarcia/pubs/n-effect.pdf); [Bai & Hew](https://hub.hku.hk/handle/10722/368255)); claims about how often to reset boards are vendor-only and unverified. [moderate]
5. **A whole-school goal instead of class-versus-class money tables**, such as a teachers' challenge unlocked when the ring passes 50%. Competition plus collaboration works best ([Sailer & Homner](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)), but the strands disagree on class contests: the leaderboard strand suggests them, while the teen-safety strand warns they turn a voluntary activity into pressure, a caution the Department's guidance supports ([Oireachtas](https://www.oireachtas.ie/en/debates/question/2018-07-03/178/)). [moderate; strands disagree]
6. **A sponsor-matched "double day" mid-campaign.** A 1:1 match is enough ([Karlan & List](https://isps.yale.edu/sites/default/files/publication/2012/12/AER%2C%202007%2C%20Karlan%2C%20DoesPriceMatter.pdf)), and the middle is where campaigns stall ([Maryland Smith](https://www.rhsmith.umd.edu/research/how-have-successful-crowdfunding-campaign)). [strong]
7. **Social information for adults only:** give parents and staff a high but true benchmark ("most parents who played chose 7 plays", only if it is true), and never tell pupils "N% of your year have played" ([Shang & Croson](https://econweb.ucsd.edu/~jandreon/PhilanthropyAndFundraising/Volume%202/27%20Shang%20Croson%202009.pdf); [Agerström et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/joepsy/v52y2016icp147-153.html)). This evidence comes from adult donors. [moderate; judgement]
8. **A share card, and a neutral default bundle:** a spoiler-free result image with initials, best light and board rank ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/05/04/wordle-new-york-times-user-growth)); keep €2 as the default, since the evidence cannot say whether pre-selecting €5 earns more ([Goswami & Urminsky](https://researchconnect.suny.edu/en/publications/when-should-the-ask-be-a-nudge-the-effect-of-default-amounts-on-c/)) and a school campaign is too small for a conclusive test. [weak to moderate]
9. **Check that first plays feel like a start.** The current curve is designed so ordinary plays "land in the hundreds"; if a staff trial shows most first plays well below the middle of the scale, soften it, because assigned difficulty motivates best when it errs easy ([Lomas et al.](https://doi.org/10.1145/3025453.3025638)). [moderate]

### Avoid

1. **Any prize, badge, board or message tied to spending**, such as most plays, biggest supporter, or money by pupil or class. [regulatory guidance; school rules]
2. **Streaks, daily-return rewards, push notifications, "come back" emails to pupils, and urgency beyond the true closing date.** Streaks work through fear of loss ([Silverman & Barasch](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/42ce576b-8e1f-429a-8541-e29e48dbcfbb/full)) and EU guidance says to switch them off for minors ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors)); deadlines shift when people give, not whether ([Damgaard & Gravert 2017](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/soceco/v66y2017icp78-87.html)). [strong; regulatory guidance]
3. **Engineered near-misses, variable speeds, rubber-banding, hidden settings, or celebration of poor results.** The persistence payoff is unproven ([Pisklak et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7214505)); the trust cost is not ([Dixon et al.](https://uwaterloo.ca/reasoning-decision-making-lab/sites/default/files/uploads/files/DixFugetal_10c.pdf); [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/input/gaming/segas-key-master-arcade-game-is-so-rigged-people-are-suing-the-company)). [strong]
4. **Random draws or chance bonuses without a lottery permit.** The s.92 exemption's 1,500-ticket cap fits badly with open-ended plays ([Irish Statute Book](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/2024/en/act/pub/0035/sec0092.html)). [law]
5. **A public feed of recent €2 payments.** One visible small gift lowered later gifts by about £5 ([Smith et al.](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/cmpo/publications/papers/2013/abstract302.html)); show counts and initials instead. [strong observational]
6. **Students' full names, photos or avatars, or public lists of the lowest ranks** ([Bai & Hew](https://hub.hku.hk/handle/10722/368255); [DPC summary](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/blogs/2022/01/the-irish-dpc-publishes-final-version-of-its-childrens-fundamentals)). [moderate; regulator guidance]
7. **"Buy now" or "get more plays" copy, "best value" badges, or token currencies aimed at pupils.** Keep euro prices side by side ([Unfair Commercial Practices Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2005/29/oj), unverified; [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/en/perspectives/2025/03/qas-on-the-eu-consumer-protection-authorities-joint-guidance-paper)). [law and regulator guidance]
8. **Design myths and attention traps:** the "80% brand recognition" claim, red for scores or rank zones, flat buttons without visible edges, parallax or auto-scrolling tickers, dark-mode body text, skeleton screens as a speed fix, and web push (2.7% average opt-in in one vendor sample) ([insights4print](https://www.insights4print.ceo/2019/02/color-increases-brand-recognition-by-80-the-real-contents-of-the-loyola-study-revealed/); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/flat-ui-less-attention-cause-uncertainty/); [Viget](https://viget.com/articles/a-bone-to-pick-with-skeleton-screens); [PushOwl](https://docs.pushowl.com/en/articles/6361107-web-push-notification-benchmarks)). [mixed]
